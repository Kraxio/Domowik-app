What these guides cover

Household matters rarely cause trouble because they are difficult. The trouble comes from information sitting in different places: the warranty in a drawer, a photo of the receipt in the gallery, the service date in someone’s memory, and the value of the equipment nowhere at all. As long as nothing happens, none of it matters.

The problem shows up at a specific moment — a flood, a fault just after the warranty ends, or a phone change where the data turns out to have stayed on the old device. These guides describe how to prepare in advance so that moment costs less than it otherwise would.

Each piece walks through one subject from start to finish: what to record, in what order, how long it takes and where people most often go wrong. No theory, and no assumption that you want to spend a weekend on it.

Who these guides are for

For people after a loss, or before one

If you have ever tried to list the contents of your home from memory, you know the list runs out sooner than it should.

For anyone who has missed a warranty

Two years is far too long to remember when you bought a washing machine. A reminder system solves that once.

For anyone changing phones

App data does not always transfer automatically. Better to know that before wiping the old device than after.

Where to start

If this is your first visit, start with the home inventory guide — a record of what you own is the foundation the other two build on. Deadlines make sense once you know which device they refer to, and a backup protects what you have already recorded.
